# Problem Statement

## District Water-Tanker System

A rapidly growing town supplies piped water to most neighbourhoods, but shortages occur during summer and in areas with weak infrastructure.
During shortages, the municipal office sends water tankers to housing societies, public schools, hospitals, and informal settlements.
The officer responsible for tanker distribution explains:

> "Every morning, we receive more requests than we can fulfil. Some are genuine emergencies. Some areas receive tankers repeatedly because they have influential representatives. Other areas wait for days.
> Residents do not know why one request was accepted and another was rejected. Drivers sometimes reach a location and discover there is no suitable place to unload the water. We also receive complaints that tankers never arrived, while the driver says the delivery was completed."

## Current Situation

The municipality has:
- 14 municipal tankers
- 11 contracted private tankers
- Six filling stations
- Approximately 80–150 requests on a normal summer day
- More than 250 requests during severe shortages
- A small dispatch team working in two shifts

Requests currently arrive through phone calls, written letters, visits to the municipal office, and referrals from elected representatives.

## Additional Constraints

- Hospitals and schools may require priority treatment
- Informal settlements often lack formal addresses
- Some neighbourhoods have private borewells but still request municipal water
- Tanker capacities differ
- Filling stations operate at different speeds and hours
- Traffic conditions strongly affect delivery time
- Drivers may have basic phones rather than smartphones
- Mobile connectivity is unreliable in some areas
- Water availability at filling stations changes during the day
- Any prioritisation process may be challenged by residents or local representatives
- Publicly revealing household-level requests may create privacy and safety concerns
- The municipality has limited authority over contracted tanker drivers
- The system must continue functioning during power or internet outages

Peak summer begins in six weeks.
